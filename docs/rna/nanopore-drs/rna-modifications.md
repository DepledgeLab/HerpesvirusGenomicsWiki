Methods for detecting RNA modifications -- including m6A, m5C, inosine, pseudouridine, and 2’O-methylation -- have improved substantially, including highly sensitive modification-specific methods such as short-read GLORI-Seq (m6A) and BACS (pseudouridine), as well as direct detection of modifications through nanopore DRS. While these approaches offer new opportunities to study RNA regulation, they remain limited by the lack of robust ground-truth datasets and standardized analytical guidelines. Readers should be aware that multiple recent benchmarking studies have determined that Dorado-based RNA modification calling is prone to high false-positive rates. These studies have generally concluded that extensive calibration, filtering, and validation by orthologous methods is absolutely essential. This should include the use of in vitro transcribed (i.e. RNA modification-free) controls, as well as the use of RNA modification installation inhibitors where possible.
